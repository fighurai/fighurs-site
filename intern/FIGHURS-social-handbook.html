What this magazine is

A magazine, not a content page.

The sentence

FIGHURS is an independent culture magazine documenting the figures rewriting fashion, internet culture, and creative identity.

It is digital and print. It spotlights trailblazing figures in fashion, literature, science, and plenty of other fields — people redefining culture and innovation their way. The magazine is here to tell their stories.

The belief under it: it is easier to compete than to appreciate. FIGHURS celebrates creativity, ambition, and impact. Not just recognition. Flowers. Well-deserved flowers, no longer a figment of their imagination. That line is from the editor. Use the feeling. Don’t paraphrase it into a slogan mill.

What you are here to do

  • Post the issues, the film, and the people in them.
  • Sound like the site: short, warm, sure, specific.
  • Send people to a real URL. Every post needs a door.
  • Credit the subject. The magazine is the host, not the star.
  • Draft freely. Publish only after the editor says yes, until you are told a post type is yours to ship.

Brand kit · name

Spell it FIGHURS.

The name

FIGHURS. One word. It is “figures,” spelled the magazine’s way. In a headline or a sentence, set it in capitals: FIGHURS.

The logo word next to the smile is always lowercase: fighurs.

Never

Figures, Figurs, Fighers, Fighur’s, FIGHERS. A YouTube title once says “MAGZINE.” That is a typo. The word is magazine.

How to refer to it

“FIGHURS,” “the magazine,” “the issue.” Don’t write “FIGHURS Magazine™ brand.” Don’t call the people in it “our influencers.” They are the figures. Use their names.

Brand kit · mark

The smile is the logo.

Official mark, from the logo file. Two dots, one smile, yellow ground, black drawing. Do not redraw it, trace it, or swap in an emoji.

Use it this way

  • Primary lockup: smile + lowercase fighurs, black type, on lime, paper, or white.
  • On magenta, cobalt, or black: the smile stays yellow with a black face. The word can go white.
  • Clear space: at least half the smile’s width on every side. Don’t crowd it into a corner with stickers.
  • Smallest useful size: about 32px tall on screen. If the eyes disappear, use the word only.
  • Files: public/brand/logo.svg on the site project, and fighurs.com/brand/logo.svg. Favicon is the same smile.
  • A yellow signature mark also lives at public/brand/signature-yellow.svg. That is a signature, not a second logo. Use it rarely, as a sign-off, and only if the editor asks.

Leave it alone

  • No gradients, glows, drop shadows, outlines, or 3D.
  • No recoloring the face. Eyes and smile stay black. The disk stays yellow.
  • No stretching, rotating, or cropping the smile into a semicircle.
  • No extra eyes, hats, tears, or “sad version.”
  • Don’t set the yellow disk straight on the lime background with no edge. Put it on paper, white, or a darker field so it reads.
  • Don’t replace it with 😊 or a different smiley from a sticker pack.

Space Mono, uppercase, tracked out, small. Eyebrows, buttons, issue numbers, nav. If Space Mono isn’t in the tool, IBM Plex Mono or a plain mono is the fallback. Do not set captions in a script, a serif, or a “luxury” Didot.

Headlines

Uppercase Helvetica, crushed together. Short. A headline is a slam: THE ICON. FLOWERS. NYFW SS26.

Body

Same Helvetica, regular, sentence case. Say the specific thing. Then stop.

Buttons and UI

Mono, uppercase, a 1px black rule, lots of letterspacing. On the site, a hover fills cobalt, magenta, or cyan. Pick one.

Brand kit · picture

The photo is the design.

Use stills from the issues and the site. Full-bleed portraits, runway, studio, collage. Faces stay large. Color is a bar, a full frame behind type, or a caption plate — not a filter on the photo.

Feed crop is 4:5. Stories and Reels are 9:16. YouTube thumbs are 16:9. Keep faces and the smile out of the bottom strip, where the app covers them.

Do not use stock photos, AI portraits, beige fashion-grid templates, crumpled-paper textures, or neon cyber gradients. A little grain is fine. The site uses a very light grain, around 7%. If you can see the noise before you see the person, it’s too much.

If a page credits a photographer, the caption credits them too. If you can’t find the credit, ask before posting.

Voice

Warm, short, sure.

The editor’s tone is an ever-smiling host who takes the work seriously and the people in it even more seriously. Smile is the temperature. It is not a bit, and it is not cute for its own sake.

How a week works

Post the magazine. Then get out of the way.

A steady week, no new issue

  1. One archive cover or still, with the issue link.
  2. One line that could only belong to that person. Pull it from the published blurb or a page you actually read.
  3. Stories: a page-flip, a still, a “read this” sticker to the issue URL. Three frames is plenty.
  4. If a film exists for that issue, one clip or a link to the interview. Point to @fighursonfilm.

Three strong posts beat seven empty ones. Skip a day rather than post a quote you made up.

Issue week

  1. Cover, full bleed. Title, number, name. Link to the flip reader.
  2. A second still the next day. Different crop, one new fact from the issue.
  3. Film or trailer the day it goes up, cover frame in the first second, link in the first line of the caption.
  4. Stories that day: cover, a favorite spread, the URL, and a tag of the subject if the editor has confirmed the handle.
  5. A week later, one “still reading” post. Not a recap thread.

Feed

Cover. Still plus one sentence. Real pull-quote, editor-approved. Film announcement. “Read it” with the flip link.

Stories

Lime, magenta, or cobalt frame. Mono label in the corner: NO. 007. Face in the middle. Link sticker to the exact issue, not the homepage, when you can.

Reels and film

First frame is a face or the cover, with the name. No cold open logo sting. End card: fighurs.com and the smile, two seconds, then out.

Caption shape

Line one is the slam. Specific, short, no hashtags yet. Then one or two sentences on who they are, using the issue’s facts. Last line is the door: the URL, written out. Tag the subject once you know the handle is right. Hashtags, if any: #FIGHURS and the name. Three tags is the ceiling. No tag stacks.

Alt text is a sentence a person can use: “Cover of FIGHURS issue 007, a portrait of Juhm.” Not a keyword list.

Comments, DMs, and the yes list

You can be kind without promising the magazine.

In the comments

  • Reply like a person at the masthead. Short. “Issue 006 is the one — link’s in the bio.”
  • If someone praises the subject, agree and name them. Don’t pivot to “follow us.”
  • If someone wants to be featured: “Features are chosen by the editor. Send the work, and don’t wait on a DM for a yes.” Never promise an issue.
  • Criticism of a subject: don’t debate, don’t pile on, don’t speak for them. Screenshot it and send it to the editor.
  • Anything legal, angry, or about money: don’t reply. Escalate.

Ask before you publish

  • Every post, until the editor clears a category for you to ship alone.
  • Any quote, even a short one.
  • Any date, price, sold-out claim, collab, gift, or “#ad.”
  • Tagging someone for the first time.
  • A photo that is not already in an issue or on fighurs.com.
  • Giveaways, follows-for-follows, comment pods, bought comments.

Passwords stay in a password manager or an official Instagram / YouTube invite. Don’t text them. Don’t put them in a caption draft.

Hard no

Don’t invent quotes, ages, cities, or credits. Don’t announce print checkout while the button says Coming soon. Don’t use the subject’s image to sell a product they didn’t agree to. Don’t post screenshots of private messages. Don’t argue with the subject in public. Don’t “fix” the logo. Don’t misspell FIGHURS. If you are unsure, the post waits.

First week

Read before you post.

  1. Watch the about reel on fighurs.com/about all the way through.
  2. Flip Issue 007 and Issue 006 in the reader. Read, don’t skim the pictures only.
  3. Watch the ICONIKKI interview.
  4. Save this page. Open both accounts. Note what’s already up so you don’t repeat last week’s cover.
  5. Draft three posts in a doc: one cover, one still, one film link. Use the captions above. Don’t schedule them.
  6. Send the drafts with the image, the URL, and who you want to tag. Wait for notes.
  7. After the first yes, post it, then check it on a phone. Face visible, name spelled right, link correct.

Send with this handbook

  • Logo: logo.svg in the site’s public/brand folder.
  • Smile favicon, same folder family.
  • Yellow signature, only if you want it used.
  • Cover files for the seven issues, or permission to pull stills from the live site and the PDFs.
  • Access via Instagram and YouTube’s own invite flow.
  • The handles you are willing to have tagged, written down, so she isn’t guessing.
